## What Stoat 0.15.5 gives us

A Sonnet agent read Stoat's source at the v0.15.5 tag without running anything. The key lines are in the appendix.

| Action | How | State in 0.15.5 |
|---|---|---|
| **Move** someone to another voice channel | Member edit with `voice_channel`, needing Move members, and Connect on the destination | **Works.** The target gets a new token in a private `UserMoveVoiceChannel` event, and everyone sees `VoiceChannelMove`. Our client doesn't handle the private event yet. |
| **Server mute, server deafen** | Member edit with `can_publish` or `can_receive`, needing Mute members or Deafen members | **Half works.** The member record changes and everyone is told, so the mark shows. The live call is updated for the moderator instead of the target. Server-side enforcement can't be had at all, even on rejoin: a token without Speak and Video carries no restriction on what may be published (architecture 5.4). |
| **Disconnect** from voice | Member edit with `remove: ["VoiceChannel"]` | **Broken.** It removes the moderator, or answers 500, and the target stays. |
| Kick, ban | Existing routes | Work, and drop the target from voice. A ban can also delete the user's messages from up to the last 7 days. |
| **Timeout** | Member edit with `timeout` | Works, with no maximum. A timed-out member can only read. |
| **Slow mode** | `slowmode`, 0 to 21,600 s, on a text channel | Works. Bypass slow mode is the exception. |
| **Deleting many messages** | `DELETE /channels/{c}/messages/bulk` | Works: up to 100, each under 7 days old, needing Manage messages. |
| **Audit log** | `GET /servers/{s}/audit_logs` | Works: 30 days kept, needing View audit logs. Each entry records a moderation action and, for edits, what changed. An `X-Audit-Log-Reason` header adds a reason. |
| Nicknames, server avatars | Member edit | Work: your own needs Change nickname or Change avatar, other people's needs Manage nicknames or Remove avatars. |
| Reports | `POST /safety/report` | **No use here.** Stored where no route reads them, so no moderator ever sees one. |

**Limits**: every `/servers/…` request, member edits and the audit log included, shares one bucket of 5 per 10 s per user and server. The client's queue waits for room, so several moves in a row take seconds.

## How voice moderation works without a Stoat fix

- **Move** uses Stoat's route as it is, and Concord clients follow the move.
- **Server mute and deafen** set the member's flags through Stoat. The target's Concord client acts on the change at once, as it already does for a mute set elsewhere:
  - a mute takes the microphone off the call and locks the button,
  - a deafen stops receiving audio and locks the button.

  Others see the mark from the same update. Stoat's own client and modified clients aren't bound by it. That's acceptable in this community, and the upstream fix closes it.
- **Disconnect** waits for the upstream fix. Doing it through LiveKit directly would mean giving `concord-web` the LiveKit secret, which can also sign tokens for any call, so anyone who took over the web server could listen in.

## Protecting server history

Asked for by Jaakko: "so we don't get random users nuking server histories". From a second source read of Stoat 0.15.5, appendix.

**What can wipe history, and who can do it**:

| Action | Needs | What goes | Undo |
|---|---|---|---|
| Delete a channel | Manage channels | Every message in it and their files, in one request. The audit log records the channel's name, not how many messages went. | None in Stoat |
| Delete messages one by one | Manage messages, or your own | The message, and its files from storage within about a minute. | None |
| Delete in bulk | Manage messages | 100 a request, as fast as 15 requests per 10 s per channel, only messages under 7 days old. The audit log keeps just a count. | None |
| Ban with deletion | Ban members | The user's messages from up to the last 7 days in every channel. | None |
| Delete the server | The owner only | Everything, its audit log included. | None |

**What can't happen**:
- An ordinary member can only delete their own messages.
- Nobody can grant a permission they don't hold themselves.
- Nobody can edit or assign a role at or above their own.

So history is wiped only by someone who was given one of the permissions above, through a mistake in roles, a stolen session, or a person who turns.

**Rules in Concord's client only stop mistakes.** Stoat checks permissions, and anyone holding one can call Stoat's API directly. So the protection comes in four layers:

1. **Fewer hands.** Concord's role editor makes the dangerous permissions hard to hand out by accident, and impossible to give to everyone.
2. **Friction.** Destructive actions in the client take a typed confirmation and a reason, and the bulk ones are for server admins only.
3. **A guard that cuts a wipe short**, even one done by script over the API. A Stoat bot account, ranked just below the owner, watches deletions. When one person passes a limit, it strips their roles and tells the owner and admins.
4. **A way back.** Messages and files are backed up hourly instead of nightly. A restore tool brings a channel's deleted messages and files back, with their original IDs and authors.

**The guard shortens a wipe; it doesn't prevent one.** A channel deletion takes one request. A bulk wipe can take 700 to 1,500 messages in a channel before the guard acts. **The backups and the restore are the real protection**, so they come first (task 10), and the guard after (task 12).

**What none of this covers**: someone holding the owner's session. They can delete the server, its audit log included, and nothing in Stoat outranks them. Only the backups help then, which is why the owner account should use TOTP.

### 2. Following a move

**Built**:
- **The controller handles `UserMoveVoiceChannel`.** It reconnects with the token Stoat sent, with no `join_call` of its own, keeping mute, deafen and volumes.
- **An expected move isn't a kick.** While a move is under way, the old room's removal doesn't read as "Disconnected". If no token comes, or it arrives after its 10 s life, the client falls back to an ordinary join.
- **Edge cases**: two moves in a row, and a move during a reconnect.
- **A toast** says who moved you and where.

**Done when** a live check moves `aino` back and forth between Olohuone and Pelaamo over the API, twice in quick succession, then once while her connection is reconnecting. Her call must follow each time without a click, send audio within 3 s, and send no extra `join_call`.

### 9. Reports

Stoat's own reports reach no one, so Concord's layer takes them.

**Built**:
- **Reporting**: "Report message" and "Report user" in their menus, with a reason from a short list and optional notes.
- **The store** keeps who reported what, when and why, and a copy of the reported message's text, so the evidence survives its deletion. That copy is the one exception to the layer's rule of storing no Stoat data. It's dropped 90 days after the report is closed.
- **Who sees them**:
  - a report from a server channel goes to that server's moderators, meaning those with Manage messages, in a "Reports" section of its server settings,
  - a report from a DM, or about a user, goes to the app's admins on `ADMIN_USER_IDS`, on a "Reports" page in the user menu until the admin panel exists.
- **Handling**: jump to the message, then close the report with a note. Closing doesn't act by itself; the moderator uses the usual actions.
- **Being told**: moderators see a count beside "Reports", fetched every minute.
- **Rules**: the reporter is never shown to the reported person. Each person may file 10 reports an hour.

**Done when** a live check has `dev` report one of aino's messages:
- `dev-admin` sees it in the server's Reports with the text, jumps to the message and closes it,
- `aino`, without Manage messages, can't list reports over the API,
- a DM report reaches the admin list, and only it.

### 10. A way back

First, because it's the only layer that undoes damage.

**Built**:
- **Measured first**: the size of the message, attachment and channel collections, and how long a dump takes inside the database container's 768 MB.
- **An hourly snapshot**, under its own restic tag:
  - a plain dump of the messages, channels, servers and attachments collections, so restic compresses and deduplicates it,
  - plus the files' storage, which restic copies incrementally, so only new files cost anything.

  48 hourly snapshots are kept beside the nightly ones. The weekly `restic check` reads a sample rather than everything.
- **`deploy/restore-history.sh`**, run as root on the VPS. Given a channel, deleted or not, and a time, it:
  - restores the nearest earlier snapshot into a temporary database and a temporary file store,
  - with `--dry-run` only reports how many messages and files it would bring back,
  - otherwise first takes a dump of the live database,
  - then inserts only the documents whose IDs are missing, so running it twice is harmless: the channel, its messages and their attachment records,
  - puts a deleted channel back in the server's channel list and its category,
  - copies the files back through the storage's own API, not into its data folder,
  - restarts nothing.

  Unread markers for a restored channel start fresh.
- **Architecture 11.3 names this tool** as the one bulk write to Stoat's database, done over SSH by an admin, with its dry run and backup.
- **The runbook** gets a "History was wiped" section: what the guard's message means, the dry run, the restore, and the check afterwards.

**Done when** a drill on the local stack:
- deletes a channel holding 200 seeded messages and 10 attachments, two of them uploaded after the last nightly snapshot, and bulk-deletes 50 messages in another channel,
- dry-runs, then restores both,
- shows a persona the same messages and files as before, after reconnecting, with the channel back in its category,
- runs the restore a second time to no effect.

### 11. Fewer hands, more friction

**Built**:
- **Dangerous permissions marked** in the role editor: Manage channels, Manage messages, Ban members, Manage server, Manage roles and Manage permissions. Each says what it lets someone destroy, for example "Delete channels, with every message in them, for good".
- **Granting one** to a role takes a confirmation that names how many members will get it.
- **@everyone can't be given any of them** in Concord's editor. A server that already gives them to everyone gets a warning at the top of Roles, with one click to remove them.
- **Suggested roles** for a new server, as starting points:
  - **Moderator**: kick, time out, voice moderation and Manage messages, but not Manage channels or Ban members,
  - **Admin**: everything. Manage server also lets someone invite bots, which Stoat can't separate, so the guard flags any other bot ranked above the moderators.
- **Deleting a channel** takes typing its name, and says its messages and files go for good. In Concord's client it's for server admins only, as bulk deletion is.
- **Reasons** for every destructive action go to the audit log.

**Done when** a live check shows:
- @everyone can't be given Manage channels in the editor,
- granting Manage messages to a role asks first and names the member count,
- deleting a channel needs its name typed, and a Moderator from the suggested roles isn't offered it.

### 12. The history guard

**Built**: a small service of our own, `guard`, in its own container with no ports open. Its Stoat bot token is in the server-only `host.env`. It isn't part of `concord-web`, because a bot that outranks the moderators shouldn't sit in the web server's process.
- **Setup**: the owner invites the guard's bot and gives it a role ranked just below their own, with View audit logs, Assign roles and Kick members. The runbook has the steps. Every 10 minutes the guard checks that its role is still the top one after the owner's, that it can still act, and that no other bot ranks above the moderators. If any check fails, it tells the owner.
- **Speed**: a deletion event makes it read the audit log at once, coalesced, so it isn't polling every 5 s. It saves the last entry it read, so a restart misses nothing.
- **Limits**, counted per person over other people's messages, channels, bans and roles:
  - more than 25 of others' messages deleted in 10 minutes, or 150 in 24 hours,
  - a second bulk deletion in 10 minutes,
  - a second channel deletion in 24 hours by anyone but the owner. The first only alerts, since the guard can't tell an empty channel from a full one,
  - more than 3 bans in 10 minutes,
  - any role deletion.

  They can be tuned in `host.env`.
- **When a limit is passed**:
  - it strips the person's roles, since Stoat won't time out anyone who can time out others,
  - it records which roles it took, so the owner can give them back with one step after a false alarm,
  - it DMs the owner and admins who did what, and which restore to run.

  The owner is never touched, and can't be.
- **If the guard is down** for more than 5 minutes, `status.sh` and the owner's DM say so.
- **Audit log retention** goes from 30 days to 365, a Revolt.toml setting.

**Done when** a live check on the local stack shows:
- a Moderator persona deleting others' messages one by one over the API is stripped before the 30th,
- a persona doing two bulk deletions is stripped after the second,
- an admin deleting one channel raises only an alert, and a second strips them,
- a false alarm is undone by the owner, with the same roles back,
- with the guard stopped mid-attack and started again, it catches up on what it missed and acts,
- a role edit that ranks something above the guard brings a warning.

## Decisions

Each is a recommendation that sign-off accepts unless you say otherwise.

1. **Server mute and deafen are enforced by Concord clients** until Stoat is fixed. They take effect at once with no reconnect. Stoat's own client and modified clients can ignore them. Server-side enforcement isn't possible on 0.15.5.
2. **No Disconnect until Stoat fixes it.** The other route needs the LiveKit secret in the web server. A moderator can drag someone into another channel meanwhile. An "AFK" voice channel to drop people into is possible too, but I'd rather not add one.
3. **Reports go through Concord's own layer**, with a copy of the message's text kept as evidence for 90 days after closing, against the layer's rule of storing no Stoat data.
4. **Server reports go to members with Manage messages, and DM and user reports to the app admins.**
5. **Dragging is an extra beside the "Move to" menu**, which is the accessible way.
   **Menus follow Discord's layout** from Jaakko's screenshots, leaving out what Stoat can't back, and the roles dialog becomes a Roles submenu.
6. **Bulk deletion is for server admins only**, at Jaakko's request: deleting many messages, and a ban's message deletion. A server admin is the owner, or someone with both Manage server and Manage messages. Stoat doesn't know this rule: it lets anyone with Manage messages delete in bulk over its API, so the rule holds in Concord's client only.
7. **Webhooks stay out.** Their permission stays hidden until bots and integrations get their own milestone.
8. **Lengths**: Discord's timeout lengths and slow mode steps, as listed.
9. **The guard is its own service**, not part of `concord-web`. It's the one new container, about 50 MB, and it holds a token that outranks the moderators, so it stays away from the web server.
10. **The guard strips roles and records them**, rather than only alerting. Stoat won't time out anyone who can time out others, so a timeout can't be the first step for moderators. A false alarm is undone in one step from the record.
11. **Moderators keep Manage messages** to delete spam. Mass deletion is caught by the guard and undone by a restore. Manage channels and Ban members are for admins, who may delete one channel a day before the guard steps in.
12. **Hourly snapshots of messages and files**, and the audit log kept a year. The most a wipe can lose for good is the last hour.
13. **Order**:
   - tasks 1 to 3 first, the voice moderation you asked for,
   - then 4, the menus, which the later tasks add their items to,
   - then 5, permissions that work, which touches everyone,
   - then 6 to 9, text moderation, the audit log and reports,
   - then 10 to 12, protecting history, the way back first,
   - then 13 and 14.

   Tasks 5 to 8 don't depend on the voice work, so a second agent could take them in parallel.

**Belongs elsewhere**, for the record:
- **Disabling an account app-wide** and **instance bans**: Stoat has no route for either, so they need the root-side runner in the admin panel's plan.
- **Automatic moderation**, such as word filters: Stoat has none, and a client-side filter would be easy to get around.

## Appendix: source notes, Stoat v0.15.5 (commit dc31dd7)

**Deletion and bots**, from a second read:
- **Messages are hard-deleted**, one by one or in bulk: `Message::delete` in `core/database/src/models/messages/model.rs`, lines 999 to 1011, and `delete_messages` in `messages/ops/mongodb.rs`, lines 301 to 312.
- **Files of a single deletion** are purged by crond within about 60 s (`file_deletion.rs`, lines 11 to 35).
- **Channel deletion** deletes all its messages and files (`channels/ops/mongodb.rs`, lines 236 to 251, and 299). The audit log records only `ChannelDelete{channel, name}`.
- **Deleting someone else's message** is audit-logged as `MessageDelete{author, channel}` with who did it (`message_delete.rs`, lines 26 to 53). Deleting your own isn't.
- **Bulk deletion** takes 100 ids, messages under 7 days old, needing Manage messages (`message_bulk_delete.rs`, lines 101 and 111). The audit log records only a count.
- **Granting**: you can't grant a permission you lack (`core/permissions/src/models/mod.rs`, lines 92 to 113), nor edit roles at or above your rank (`roles_edit.rs`, line 45).
- **Bots**: anyone with Manage server can invite one (`bots/invite.rs`, lines 28 to 45). A bot receives the deletion events, may read the audit log, and follows the same rank rules.
- **The audit log** can be polled with `after=<id>` and a limit of up to 100. Its retention is `[api.audit_logs] expires_after`.
